about

Press Print, pick a film, take the picture.

The look is applied inside the camera, while the photo is being made. What lands on the card is the finished JPEG, no app or editing afterwards.

Why this exists.

I shoot a lot of film, but I’ve always loved the unique character of early digital CCD sensors. My 20-year-old IXUS 60 is probably my absolute favorite camera—it’s been with me forever and holds countless memories.

When I realized what was actually possible with CHDK under the hood, I was completely hooked. I started using AI as a coding partner to build the UI, refine the script logic, and bring in features inspired by modern cameras—like Auto-ISO with a minimum shutter speed and Fujifilm's JPEG film simulations, matching the color output against original Fuji files using custom YUV matrices and WB shifts.

What started as a private experiment for my own IXUS grew into EXCCD. I cleaned up the code and put it online, and I’d be genuinely thrilled if others have fun reviving their old digicams with it, help port it to more models, and share their own recipes along the way.

Massive respect to the CHDK developers—their open-source work over the years is the sole reason any of this is possible.

  1. Pick looks

    On this page you choose which films you want on your camera. Your browser packs them into one ZIP file; nothing gets uploaded anywhere.

  2. Unpack to the SD card

    EXCCD lives on the SD card and starts from there. The camera's own software isn't touched: take the card out and it's a normal Canon again.

  3. Press Print, shoot

    Each look sets Canon's own colour settings and bends the raw sensor data with a tone curve, before the camera turns it into a JPEG. That's why it behaves like a camera setting, not like a filter on top.

Public beta

Work in progress, and that's fine.

EXCCD is a hobby project, and it isn't finished. I use it on my IXUS 60 all the time, but every camera model is a bit different inside: another sensor, another colour matrix (the maths that turns sensor values into colours), another firmware. So whenever real photos from a camera come in, I re-tune the looks, and things will keep changing between versions.

  • Tested – IXUS 60 / SD600. Used day to day; the newest features are still being checked.
  • In testing – IXUS 132 / ELPH 115 IS and PowerShot SX110 IS. Built and partly tested on real cameras.
  • Ported, not tested – IXUS 145 / ELPH 135. Built, but nobody has run it on a real camera yet.

You can only download for cameras that are ready for it. Some sample photos are simulated on a PC from other shots until I've taken real ones with that camera; those are marked “simulated”.

Free and open source

It's free.

There's nothing to pay, and there won't be. I built this for fun and for my own camera.

The code is GPL v2, the same licence as CHDK, so anyone can read it, change it and pass it on. The looks themselves (curves, labels, film table) are CC BY-SA 4.0. Everything is on GitHub. If you find a bug, have an idea or want to help bring it to another camera, I'm happy to hear from you.

Keeping it simple

Still a point-and-shoot.

CHDK can do a lot, and you see that in its menus: hundreds of settings. For EXCCD I only kept what I actually use when I'm out shooting. You pick a film, the screen stays clean, and everything else waits in a short options menu.

  1. One button

    Print opens the film selection, the arrow keys flip through the films, FUNC/SET picks one. That's all you need to know to get started.

  2. A clean screen

    On screen there's only the battery and the name of the film. When you half-press the shutter or open Canon's menus or FUNC/SET, both get out of the way and come back afterwards.

  3. Films, not parameters

    You pick a film by its label, a bit like grabbing a roll from the fridge. Contrast, colour and tone curve come with it, so you don't have to set them yourself.

  4. Canon underneath

    Canon's menus, modes and buttons work as before. Pick Off and you get the camera's normal colours.

White balance

Your colours, set on the camera.

On film, the colour of the light is part of the look: daylight film under a lamp turns warm, in the shade it turns blue. EXCCD lets you play with that the way a Fujifilm camera does, in two layers.

  1. White balance

    First you set the camera's own white balance under MENU → Look: Auto, Daylight, Cloudy, Tungsten, Fluorescent or Custom. That's Canon's normal setting, just moved next to the film.

  2. WB shift

    On top of that you nudge the colour on a grid, like the white balance shift on a Fujifilm camera: left and right go from cyan to red, up and down between blue and yellow, nine steps each way. A few steps towards red and yellow make a look warmer, towards blue it gets cooler.

  3. Change it any time

    Both live on the camera, not in the download, so you can change them whenever you like. The shift is worked into the look's tone curve, so it doesn't slow the camera down.

  4. For the curious

    One step is roughly 3 % more or less red or blue in the raw data. It isn't calibrated yet, so take the numbers as a feel, not a measurement. You see the shift in the photo, not on the screen, and with the film set to Off it does nothing.

Recipes

Save it as a recipe.

If you've shot Fujifilm, you know recipes: a film simulation plus a handful of settings that together make a look of your own. What always bugged me on my Fuji is that the white balance isn't part of it. In EXCCD it is.

  1. What's in a recipe

    The film, white balance, WB shift, Dynamic+, colour noise filter, B&W filter and all effects. In short: everything under MENU → Look.

  2. What isn't

    Shutter speed, focus, zoom, flash, ND filter, RAW and double exposure. Those depend on the moment, not on the look, so a recipe leaves them alone.

  3. Save and switch

    Set everything up, then Look → Save as recipe and pick one of four slots, C1 to C4. To switch, go to Recipe at the top of the menu and flip with left and right. If you change something afterwards, a * after the slot name tells you.

  4. Share them

    Recipes live in a plain text file on the card, CHDK/FILMS/RECIPES.TXT. On the PC you can rename them, write new ones or pass them on; the file explains its own format. Saving on the camera rewrites the file, so your own comments in it get lost.

RAW develop

Change your mind later.

A DNG is the raw sensor data, before the camera has decided anything about colour or contrast. If you save one next to each JPEG, you can develop the same photo again later, with another film, right on the camera. A bit like taking the negative back to the lab and asking for a different print.

  1. Shoot with DNG

    Turn on MENU → RAW → Save RAW. From then on every photo also lands on the card as a DNG, next to the JPEG.

  2. Pick photo and film

    Later, choose RAW → RAW develop, pick the DNG and the film you want, and effects if you like.

  3. Take one helper shot

    Press the shutter once, at anything. Canon's JPEG engine only runs after a shot, so the camera needs one to do the work. You get a new JPEG; the DNG stays untouched.

White balance, Dynamic+ and the date stamp come from the original photo. The Exif data (date, exposure, orientation) comes from the helper shot, so hold the camera the same way and don't use digital zoom. Developing takes a few seconds, longer with heavy effects; a bar shows how far it is.

Also in there

A few extras.

All optional, and all in the options menu (MENU → Look → Effects). Like the looks, they're applied to the raw sensor data before the camera makes the JPEG. So grain or a light leak becomes part of the photo, the way it would on film.

Effects

  • Black Mist

    A soft glow around bright areas, like a diffusion filter in front of the lens. It takes the hard edge off digital sharpness.

    Off · 1/8 · 1/4

  • Halation

    A red-orange halo around bright lights. On film without an anti-halation layer, light bounces back from the film base and glows red around lamps and bright edges.

    Off · On

  • Vignette

    Darker corners, like on old or simple lenses.

    Off · Light · Strong

  • Light Leak

    A warm streak of light from the left or right edge, like on a film camera with a leaky back. Side and position change from shot to shot.

    Off · On

  • Date stamp

    Orange digits in the corner, like the date stamp on 90s film compacts: '26 10 31

    Off · On

  • Double exposure

    Two shots on one frame. The second one is mixed into the first in the raw data, much like exposing a negative twice.

    Off · On

  • Grain

    Film grain, added to the raw data. Unlike coloured digital noise it has one colour, comes in clumps and is strongest in the midtones, the way real grain behaves. On Auto it follows the speed (ISO) of the film you picked.

    Off · Auto · Fine · Medium · Coarse

  • Film frame

    A thin black border around the picture, like a print from the lab. It covers the outer edge of the image, so leave a little room when you frame.

    Off · Thin

Shooting

  • Auto-ISO with a shutter limit

    Canon's Auto ISO tends to keep the ISO low and the shutter slow, so indoor photos get blurry. Here you set the slowest time you accept, like the minimum shutter speed setting on many newer cameras. The camera raises the ISO only when it has to, up to a maximum you choose. On Auto the limit follows the zoom: about 1 / focal length, never slower than 1/30. It steps in when the camera's ISO is on Auto and the flash doesn't fire.

    Off · Auto · 1/8 … 1/500 · max. ISO 200 … 1600

  • Dynamic+

    For bright scenes: the camera exposes a little shorter so the sky doesn't burn out, and the curve brings the shadows back up.

    Off · +1 · +2

  • Zone focus

    Focus at a fixed distance instead of autofocus. Handy on the street: the camera fires right away instead of hunting for focus first.

    AF · 0.5 m … ∞

  • Shutter speed

    Set the exposure time yourself, for long night shots or to freeze movement.

    Auto · 16″ … 1/1000

  • ND filter

    Many of these cameras have a small grey filter inside that cuts the light, often instead of a real aperture. Here you swing it in or out yourself.

    Auto · In · Out

  • Flash power

    A fixed flash strength instead of automatic. Low is nice for a bit of fill light.

    Auto · Low · Mid · High

  • B&W colour filter

    Works like a coloured filter on the lens in black-and-white photography: yellow, orange or red darken blue skies and make clouds stand out.

    Off · Yellow · Orange · Red

  • Colour noise filter

    Small sensors make coloured blotches in dark areas. This smooths them out and leaves the fine grey noise alone, which looks more like grain.

    Off · Auto · Light · Strong

  • Raw + film log

    Saves a DNG next to each JPEG if you want one. FILMLOG.CSV lists every shot with the film you used.

    Off · DNG

Firmware version

How do I find my firmware version?

With the installer (Windows): start the EXCCD installer, choose your camera, insert a card formatted in the camera and click Check firmware…. It tells you what to do next.

By hand:

  1. Download ver.req and vers.req and copy both to the top level of the SD card. Older models read the first one, newer ones the second.
  2. Put the card in the camera (not locked) and switch it on in playback mode.
  3. Hold FUNC/SET and press DISP. The camera shows a line like Firmware Ver GM1.00D. The letter at the end is the one that counts.
  4. Delete both files from the card afterwards.
02

Looks

Every look comes with two Dynamic+ variants (DR+1, DR+2) for bright scenes; they're always included. The small graph on each card is the look's tone curve: the dashed line means “no change”, above it the picture gets brighter, below it darker. White balance and the WB shift aren't set here but on the camera, so you can change them any time without downloading again. If you like a combination, save it as a recipe.

01

Install, step by step

Takes about ten minutes. The camera's firmware isn't changed; everything happens on the SD card. Instructions for · other camera

You need

  • the camera, with a charged battery
  • an SD card
  • a card reader and a Windows PC (macOS and Linux: by hand)

  1. Check the firmware

    EXCCD has to know where things sit inside the camera's software, and that's different for every firmware version. So it only runs on firmware of this camera. Not sure which one you have? The installer can check it (Check firmware…), or see how to find it by hand.

  2. Download your looks

    Pick your films on the looks page and download the ZIP. Just leave it in your Downloads folder; the installer looks for it there.

  3. Format the card in the camera

    Use the camera's own Format menu, not the PC, so the card is set up the way the camera expects it. This erases the card, so copy your photos off first.

  4. Run the installer

    Installer for Windows

    Choose your camera, check that it picked your download and the right card, then click Install EXCCD on the card. It only lists SD cards, so it can't touch your other drives. It keeps your photos and makes the card bootable, which means the camera can start EXCCD from it.

    Windows warns about an unknown app because the installer isn't signed. Signing costs money every year, and this is a free hobby project; the code is open on GitHub. Click More info → Run anyway. It asks for administrator rights because it writes the card's boot sector.

  5. Lock the card

    Slide the card's write-protect switch to LOCK and put it in the camera. It sounds odd, but on these cameras that's the signal to start EXCCD from the card. Photos are still saved as usual.

  6. Switch on, pick a look

    In shooting mode press Print, flip through the films with the arrow keys and confirm with FUNC/SET. The film you're shooting is shown at the bottom of the screen.

Without the installer (macOS, Linux)
  1. Format the card in the camera.
  2. Unpack the ZIP to the top level of the card, so that DISKBOOT.BIN and the CHDK folder sit right there, not inside another folder.
  3. Eject the card and put it in the camera unlocked. Switch on in playback mode and choose MENU → Firm Update. That starts EXCCD until you switch off. To make it start on its own, run Make Card Bootable once from the EXCCD menu (MENU → System), then lock the card.
  4. This camera only starts EXCCD from a bootable card, so the card has to be made bootable once, for example with the tools from CHDK's guide Prepare your SD card. Then lock the card.
02

If it doesn't work

The camera starts normally, EXCCD doesn't show up
  • Is the card locked? Without LOCK the camera ignores EXCCD.
  • Is the card too big? See You need above.
  • Was the card formatted in the camera and then installed with the installer (or made bootable)?
  • Does the firmware match? Other versions simply won't start.
Windows won't run the installer

SmartScreen blocks unsigned programs at first: click More info → Run anyway. If your antivirus removes it, you can check the source code and the build log on GitHub, or install by hand.

The installer doesn't find my card

It only shows removable drives. Use a card reader, close Explorer windows that show the card and click Refresh. Some built-in laptop readers report cards as fixed disks; a cheap USB reader helps.

A look doesn't match the samples

That's the beta part. Light, white balance and the camera model all change how a look turns out, and I'm still tuning the looks camera by camera. Please tell me, ideally with the photo, the look and your camera.

How do I get the normal camera back?

Unlock the card or take it out. To remove EXCCD for good, format the card in the camera.

03

Questions

What is CHDK?

CHDK (Canon Hack Development Kit) is a free add-on for Canon compacts, made by a community of volunteers since 2007. It loads from the SD card when the camera starts and adds things Canon never built in, like raw files, manual controls and scripts. EXCCD is built on top of it: CHDK with the film looks added and a much shorter menu.

Can this damage my camera?

EXCCD runs from the SD card, like CHDK, and doesn't change the camera's firmware. Without the card, the camera behaves exactly as before. Still, it's third-party software, so use it at your own risk.

Do I lose anything compared to the normal camera?

No. Pick Off in the film selection and the camera uses its normal colours again. Canon's menus and modes stay as they are.

How is this different from a filter app?

A filter app works on the finished JPEG, so it starts from a picture the camera has already processed and compressed. EXCCD steps in earlier: the tone curve goes onto the sensor's raw data, together with Canon's own colour settings, and then the camera makes the JPEG as usual. The look is in the photo from the start, and there's nothing to apply afterwards.

Can I keep a raw file?

Yes. CHDK can save a raw file (DNG) next to each JPEG. That's the unprocessed sensor data, which you can develop later on a PC, or on the camera with RAW develop. Turn it on in the options (MENU → RAW).

Which look did I use for a photo?

EXCCD writes every shot with its look to A/CHDK/FILMLOG.CSV on the card.

How do I update, or add looks later?

Come back, pick a new set, download it and run the installer again with the card unlocked. It replaces the CHDK folder and keeps FILMLOG.CSV and your photos. By hand: delete the old CHDK folder first, then unpack the new ZIP.

Which languages are the menus in?

English. German is on the card too: MENU → System → Language... → german.lng. The film labels stay in English.

What about other cameras?

I'd like to bring EXCCD to more old Canon compacts: CCD models first, later maybe CMOS ones too. Every model has to be ported (finding where things live in its firmware) and then tested on a real camera, so it takes a while. If you have one, you can help.

Is it finished?

No, it's a public beta. It works, but looks get re-tuned and features change as photos from more cameras come in. Your chosen look is saved by its name, so an update won't mix it up.

Is it open source?

Yes. The code is GPL v2 like CHDK, the looks (curves, labels, film table) are CC BY-SA 4.0. Everything is on GitHub.

04

Help out

I'd love for EXCCD to grow beyond my own IXUS. Every bit of feedback helps, I'm curious about your recipes, and the project is open for more camera models.

  • Tell me what happened

    Something broken, odd colours, an idea? Open an issue on GitHub. Camera, firmware, EXCCD version and a photo (of the display, too) help me a lot to figure out what's going on.

  • Test on your camera

    Have an IXUS 132, IXUS 145 or SX110 IS, or another old Canon that CHDK supports? Real photos from real cameras are what I tune the looks with.

  • Send a pull request

    Fixes, ports and new looks are welcome. The porting guide explains what a new camera needs.
