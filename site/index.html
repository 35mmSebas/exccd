about

Press Print, pick a film, take the picture.

The look is applied inside the camera, so the JPEG on the card is already the finished photo.

Why this exists.

Old Canon digicams have a look of their own, and many of them are still lying in drawers, working perfectly. I shoot a lot of analog film, so I wanted these cameras to get a fresh set of colours, tuned against reference shots.

It started with one camera: my 20-year-old IXUS 60, my favorite camera for a long time (and I have many), with countless cherished memories on it. Instead of keeping the custom matrices and scripts to myself, I cleaned everything up, made it work on more models and put it online for anyone who enjoys shooting on old digicams. Have fun with it.

Also, credit to the CHDK developers—without their open-source work over the years, this wouldn't be possible.

  1. Pick looks

    Choose the films you want on the card. This page puts them into one ZIP, in your browser.

  2. Unpack to the SD card

    EXCCD runs from the card. Nothing is written to the camera; without the card it is a stock Canon again.

  3. Press Print, shoot

    Each look sets Canon's own colour settings and applies a tone curve to the raw data before the JPEG is made.

Public beta

Work in progress, and that's fine.

EXCCD is a hobby project, not a finished product. I use it on my IXUS 60 all the time, but every old camera model is a little different: another sensor, another colour matrix, another firmware. So the looks get re-tuned whenever real photos from a camera come in, and things will keep changing between versions.

  • Tested – IXUS 60 / SD600. Used day to day; the newest features are still being checked.
  • In testing – IXUS 132 / ELPH 115 IS and PowerShot SX110 IS. Built and partly tested on real cameras.
  • Ported, not tested – IXUS 145 / ELPH 135. Built, but nobody has run it on a real camera yet.

The download only offers cameras that are ready for it. Some sample photos are simulated on a PC until I've shot real ones; they're marked as such.

Free and open source

It's free.

EXCCD is free to download and use. It's a fun project, so there's nothing to pay.

The code is GPL v2 like CHDK, the looks (curves, labels, film table) are CC BY-SA 4.0. Everything is on GitHub. Feedback, bug reports and pull requests are welcome, and so is help with more camera models.

Keeping it simple

Still a point-and-shoot.

CHDK can do a lot, and its menus show it. For EXCCD I only kept what I actually use when I'm out shooting: one button, a clean screen and films instead of settings. The rest sits in a short options menu.

  1. One button

    Print opens the film selection, the arrow keys flip through it, FUNC/SET picks the film. That's it.

  2. A clean screen

    Only the battery and the name of the film. On half-press, in Canon's menus and in FUNC/SET both disappear and come back afterwards.

  3. Films, not parameters

    You pick a film by its label, a bit like grabbing a roll from the fridge. Contrast, colour and curve come with it.

  4. Canon underneath

    Canon's menus, modes and buttons work as before. Choose Off and you get Canon's own colours.

Also in there

A few extras.

All optional, in the options menu (MENU). Like the looks, the effects are applied to the raw sensor data before the camera makes the JPEG.

Effects

  • Black Mist

    A soft glow around the highlights, like a mist filter.

    Off · 1/8 · 1/4

  • Halation

    A red-orange halo around bright lights, as on film without an anti-halation layer.

    Off · On

  • Vignette

    Darker corners.

    Off · Light · Strong

  • Light Leak

    A warm streak from the left or right edge. The side and position change from shot to shot.

    Off · On

  • Date stamp

    Orange seven-segment digits in the corner, like on 90s compacts: '26 10 31

    Off · On

  • Double exposure

    Two shots on one frame, mixed in the raw data like two exposures on a negative.

    Off · On

  • Grain

    Real film grain in the raw data: one colour, in clumps, strongest in the midtones. On Auto it follows the speed of the film.

    Off · Auto · Fine · Medium · Coarse

  • Film frame

    A thin black border around the picture, like a print from the lab. It covers the edge of the picture, so leave some room.

    Off · Thin

Shooting

  • RAW develop

    Render a saved DNG again with another look, right on the camera. Pick the file and the look, then take one helper shot. The original stays untouched.

    Any saved DNG

  • Dynamic+

    Exposes shorter and lifts the shadows with the curve, so bright skies keep more detail.

    Off · +1 · +2

  • Zone focus

    A fixed distance instead of autofocus.

    AF · 0.5 m … ∞

  • Shutter speed

    Set the exposure time by hand.

    Auto · 16″ … 1/1000

  • ND filter

    Move the built-in ND filter in or out by hand.

    Auto · In · Out

  • Flash power

    A fixed flash strength instead of automatic.

    Auto · Low · Mid · High

  • B&W colour filter

    Yellow, orange or red filter for the black-and-white look. Darkens blue skies.

    Off · Yellow · Orange · Red

  • Colour noise filter

    Smooths out the coloured blotches of the small sensor and leaves the fine luminance noise alone.

    Off · Auto · Light · Strong

  • Raw + film log

    Saves a DNG next to each JPEG if you want one. FILMLOG.CSV lists every shot with its film.

    Off · DNG

Camera

Which camera do you have?

Beta: EXCCD is built for this camera, but it hasn't been fully tested on one yet. Expect rough edges, and please tell me how it goes on GitHub.

Firmware version

How do I find my firmware version?

With the installer (Windows): start the EXCCD installer, choose your camera, insert a card formatted in the camera and click Check firmware…. It tells you what to do next.

By hand:

  1. Download ver.req and vers.req and copy both to the top level of the SD card. Older models read the first one, newer ones the second.
  2. Put the card in the camera (not locked) and switch it on in playback mode.
  3. Hold FUNC/SET and press DISP. The camera shows a line like Firmware Ver GM1.00D. The letter at the end is the one that counts.
  4. Delete both files from the card afterwards.
02

Looks

Every look comes with two Dynamic+ variants (DR+1, DR+2): the camera exposes shorter to protect the highlights and the curve lifts the shadows. They are always included. The small graph shows each look's tone curve; the dashed line is neutral. The white balance shift moves R from cyan (−) to red (+) and B from yellow (−) to blue (+). It is built into the look's curve, so the camera needs nothing extra. The sample photos show roughly what it does.

01

Install, step by step

About ten minutes, no camera firmware is changed. Instructions for · other camera

You need

  • the camera, with a charged battery
  • an SD card
  • a card reader and a Windows PC (macOS and Linux: by hand)

  1. Check the firmware

    EXCCD only runs on firmware of this camera. Not sure which one you have? The installer can check it (Check firmware…), or see how to find it by hand.

  2. Download your looks

    Pick your films on the looks page and download the ZIP. Leave it in your Downloads folder; the installer finds it there.

  3. Format the card in the camera

    Use the camera's own Format menu, not the PC. This erases the card, so copy your photos off first.

  4. Run the installer

    Installer for Windows

    Choose your camera, check that it picked your download and the right card, then click Install EXCCD on the card. It only lists SD cards, keeps your photos and makes the card bootable.

    Windows warns about an unknown app because the installer isn't signed (signing costs money; the code is open). Click More info → Run anyway. It asks for administrator rights to write the card's boot sector.

  5. Lock the card

    Slide the card's write-protect switch to LOCK and put it in the camera. That's what makes EXCCD start on its own. The camera still saves photos.

  6. Switch on, pick a look

    In shooting mode press Print, flip with the arrow keys, confirm with FUNC/SET. The active look is shown at the bottom of the screen.

Without the installer (macOS, Linux)
  1. Format the card in the camera.
  2. Unpack the ZIP to the top level of the card, so that DISKBOOT.BIN and the CHDK folder sit right there, not inside another folder.
  3. Eject the card and put it in the camera unlocked. Switch on in playback mode and choose MENU → Firm Update. That starts EXCCD until you switch off. To make it start on its own, run Make Card Bootable once from the EXCCD menu (MENU → System), then lock the card.
  4. This camera only starts EXCCD from a bootable card, so the card has to be made bootable once, for example with the tools from CHDK's guide Prepare your SD card. Then lock the card.
02

If it doesn't work

The camera starts normally, EXCCD doesn't show up
  • Is the card locked? Without LOCK the camera ignores EXCCD.
  • Is the card too big? See You need above.
  • Was the card formatted in the camera and then installed with the installer (or made bootable)?
  • Does the firmware match? Other versions simply won't start.
Windows won't run the installer

SmartScreen blocks unsigned programs at first: click More info → Run anyway. If your antivirus removes it, you can check the source code and the build log on GitHub, or install by hand.

The installer doesn't find my card

It only shows removable drives. Use a card reader, close Explorer windows that show the card and click Refresh. Some built-in laptop readers report cards as fixed disks; a cheap USB reader helps.

A look doesn't match the samples

That's the beta part. Light, white balance and the camera model all play a role, and the looks are still being tuned per camera. Please tell me, ideally with the photo, the look and your camera.

How do I get the normal camera back?

Unlock the card or take it out. To remove EXCCD for good, format the card in the camera.

03

Questions

Can this damage my camera?

EXCCD is built on CHDK, which the CHDK community has been developing for Canon compacts since 2007. It runs from the SD card and does not change the camera's firmware. Without the card, the camera behaves exactly as before. Still, it's third-party software: use it at your own risk.

Do I lose anything compared to the normal camera?

No. Choose Off in the film selection and the camera uses Canon's own colours. Canon's menus and modes stay as they are.

How is this different from a filter app?

The tone curve is applied to the sensor's raw data before the camera makes the JPEG, together with Canon's own colour settings. The look is part of the picture the camera saves; there is nothing to apply afterwards.

Can I keep a raw file?

Yes. CHDK can save a raw file (DNG) next to each JPEG. Turn it on in the options (MENU).

Which look did I use for a photo?

EXCCD writes every shot with its look to A/CHDK/FILMLOG.CSV on the card.

How do I update, or add looks later?

Come back, pick a new set, download it and run the installer again with the card unlocked. It replaces the CHDK folder and keeps FILMLOG.CSV and your photos. By hand: delete the old CHDK folder first, then unpack the new ZIP.

Which languages are the menus in?

English. German is on the card too: MENU → System → Language... → german.lng. The film labels stay in English.

What about other cameras?

I'd like to bring EXCCD to more old Canon compacts: CCD models first, later maybe CMOS ones too. Each one has to be ported and tested on a real camera, so it takes a while. If you have one, you can help.

Is it finished?

No, it's a public beta. It works, but looks get re-tuned and features change as photos from more cameras come in. Your chosen look is stored by name, so updates don't mix it up.

Is it open source?

Yes. The code is GPL v2 like CHDK, the looks (curves, labels, film table) are CC BY-SA 4.0. Everything is on GitHub.

04

Help out

EXCCD grows with the people who use it. Every bit of feedback helps, and the project is open for more camera models.

  • Tell me what happened

    Something broken, odd colours, an idea? Open an issue on GitHub. Camera, firmware, EXCCD version and a photo (of the display, too) make it much easier.

  • Test on your camera

    Have an IXUS 132, IXUS 145 or SX110 IS, or another old Canon that CHDK supports? Real photos from real cameras are what the looks are tuned with.

  • Send a pull request

    Fixes, ports and new looks are welcome. The porting guide shows what a new camera needs.
